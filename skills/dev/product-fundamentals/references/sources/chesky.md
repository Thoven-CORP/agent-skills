# Brian Chesky — source library
Verified Chesky quotes, organised by grill move, for `product-fundamentals`. Primary = his own words in a transcript or video I read (YouTube captions are auto-generated: words verbatim, punctuation mine). Secondhand = someone else's page, or his words quoted inside an article. Where a page blocked fetching, the link is still the canonical URL.

### The 11-star ladder (climb, then come back to the reasonable rung)
Level: 4
- "The point of the process is that maybe 9, 10, 11 are not feasible. But if you go through the crazy exercise of "keep going," there's some sweet spot between "they showed up and they opened the door" and "I went to space."" — [Masters of Scale, with Reid Hoffman](https://mastersofscale.com/brian-chesky/), 2017-05-03 — primary (transcript)
- "You have to almost design the extreme to come backwards. Suddenly, doesn't knowing my preferences and having a surfboard in the house seem not crazy but reasonable?" — same episode — primary (transcript)
- "a five-star mostly means like nothing bad happened and we thought well what if there was like a six star" — [Stanford GSB talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V6h_EDcj12k), 2023-02-27 — primary (video captions)

Ask it as: "Write the 5-star lesson (nothing went wrong) for the parent, the child and the teacher. Now the 7-star and the 10-star: which rung would we be glad we built in hindsight?" "What is the 'Elon takes you to space' version of the first lesson, and which part of it can one teacher do by hand?"

### Snow White: storyboard the frames, then ask which frame a thing serves
Level: 3
- "I thought to myself what if we storyboard the perfect experience for the Airbnb … pick one frame of the experience so the moment of truth in Airbnb is when you check in right you're like is this going to be what I think it is" — [Stanford GSB talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V6h_EDcj12k), 2023-02-27 — primary (video captions; "…" marks a cut)
- "When you have to storyboard something, the more realistic it is, the more decisions you have to make." — [Fast Company, "How Snow White Helped Airbnb's Mobile Mission"](https://www.fastcompany.com/3002813/how-snow-white-helped-airbnbs-mobile-mission/), 2012-11-08 — secondhand (quoted in article; the project began from a list of the emotional moments of a stay)
- "Whenever there's a question about what should be a priority, we ask ourselves which frame will this product or idea serve." — Nathan Blecharczyk, [Sequoia, "Visualizing the Customer Experience"](https://articles.sequoiacap.com/visualizing-customer-experience), undated — secondhand (cofounder; 15 guest frames and 15 host frames)

Ask it as: "Draw the flow as about 15 frames each for the parent, the child and the teacher. What does each person feel in each frame, and which frame does this feature serve?" "Which frames happen offline (practice at home, the lesson room) that our product never touches?"

### Design the perfect experience for one person by hand, then scale it
Level: 3
- "There's really two stages of a startup's product. The first is, design a perfect experience and then you scale that experience." — [Masters of Scale](https://mastersofscale.com/brian-chesky/), 2017-05-03 — primary (transcript)
- "if you design something keep iterating until they love it and don't stop improving until that one person loves it" — [Stanford GSB talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V6h_EDcj12k), 2023-02-27 — primary (video captions)
- "We had a saying that you would do everything by hand until it was painful. So Joe and I would photograph homes until it was painful, then we get other photographers." — [Masters of Scale](https://mastersofscale.com/brian-chesky/), 2017-05-03 — primary (transcript)

Ask it as: "Which one real family and one real teacher can we run the whole flow for by hand this month? Does that first child love it before we want a second?" "What do we do by hand until it hurts, and what is the first thing we hand to software?"

### The "tell everyone" question (and 100 who love you)
Level: 1
- "What can we do to surprise you? What can we do, not to make this better, but to make you tell everyone about it?" — [Masters of Scale](https://mastersofscale.com/brian-chesky/), 2017-05-03 — primary (transcript)
- "focus on 100 people that love you rather than getting a million people that kind of like you" — [Stanford GSB talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V6h_EDcj12k), 2023-02-27 — primary (video captions; he credits Paul Graham's whiteboard advice)
- "when people love your service they become your marketing department they tell other people" — same talk — primary (video captions)

Ask it as: "Ask a parent not 'what would make this better' but 'what would we have to do for you to tell every parent you know?' What do they say, and what does a teacher say?" "Which 100 families would be devastated if this vanished, and what do they have in common?"

### The roadmap lives in the user's head
Level: 1
- "The roadmap often exists in the minds of the users you're designing things for." — [Masters of Scale](https://mastersofscale.com/brian-chesky/), 2017-05-03 — primary (transcript; a host handed them a binder of dozens of pages of notes)

Ask it as: "Which parent or teacher has already written us the long list of what they need? What is on it, and which line do all of them repeat?"

### Trust between strangers is designed
Level: 1
- "how do you know that somebody's legitimate? How do you know they're going to provide a good experience?" — [YC Startup School talk](https://jacquesmattheij.com/startup-school-2010/brian-chesky/), talk 2010-10-16 (transcript posted 2011-04-02) — primary (transcript)
- "I think we thought of it as a design problem. How do you design something for people to kind of virtually get to know people well enough to be able to trust each other, to give each other access?" — [Sequoia Crucible Moments](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/crucible-moments-airbnb), 2023-09-07 — primary (transcript)
- "But the more things you verify, the more things you inspect, the more things you certify, the fewer areas there will be to exploit a company." — [Decoder, The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/24279570/airbnb-ceo-brian-chesky-founder-mode-apple-steve-jobs-management-decoder-podcast-2024), 2024-10-28 — primary (transcript)

Ask it as: "A parent is handing a stranger access to their child. What must they see in the first minute to believe the teacher is who they say? What do we verify ourselves rather than leave to reviews?" "What would the teacher need to believe about the parent?"

### Story first: marketing and product are one job
Level: 1
- "And when we're working on a launch, one of the first things we'll do is start figuring out what the story is. And the story will often dictate the product." — [Lenny's Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4ef0juAMqoE), 2023-11-12 — primary (transcript)
- "You can't build a product unless you know how to talk about the product." — same episode — primary (transcript)
- "So the first thing is that we actually like to start a lot of product development, not with design, but with marketing. We want [our marketers] to actually have a vision and to figure out how they can tell a story." — [Figma Config 2023 conversation](https://www.figma.com/blog/config-brian-chesky-airbnb/), 2023-08-10 — primary (Figma's published transcript)

Ask it as: "Write the sentence one parent sends another about this, before any spec. What product does that sentence force us to build?" "Tell it from the teacher's side and the child's side: which of them has no story yet?"

### Fewer things, done better: the roadmap squeeze
Level: cut
- "I like that constraint. I like the constraint of anyone can't just do anything because now we're focused, now we're prioritized, now we only do things that are differentiated." — [Decoder, The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/24279570/airbnb-ceo-brian-chesky-founder-mode-apple-steve-jobs-management-decoder-podcast-2024), 2024-10-28 — primary (transcript)
- "And I said, okay, we can do about 20% of these things." — [Lenny's Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4ef0juAMqoE), 2023-11-12 — primary (transcript; after everyone wrote down everything in flight)
- "I mean, basically I spent 17 years with our team trying to do one thing." — [Masters of Scale Summit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jo4Ze2c_hAY), 2025-10-16 — primary (video captions)

Ask it as: "List everything in this idea. If we can only do 20%, which fifth survives, and what is the one thing we would spend years on?" "What goes in Cut this round, and whom does that disappoint?"

### Add a zero, then simplify
Level: cut
- "And one of the best ways to get unstuck from a problem is to imagine a 10X scale or 10X better or 10X faster where you can't do the current process to do it." — [Lenny's Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4ef0juAMqoE), 2023-11-12 — primary (transcript)
- "I used to think simplifying was removing things. What Jony and Hiroki at Apple taught me is that's not what simplifying is about; simplifying is distilling something to its essence, and to distill something to its essence, you have to deeply understand it." — [Figma Config 2023 conversation](https://www.figma.com/blog/config-brian-chesky-airbnb/), 2023-08-10 — primary (Figma's published transcript)
- "And he added a zero at the end of $5,000 and he changed it to $50,000." — [Sequoia Crucible Moments](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/crucible-moments-airbnb), 2023-09-07 — primary (transcript; Marc Andreessen on the host guarantee)

Ask it as: "Add a zero to the target (10x the families): what must change that the current process cannot do? Then, in one sentence, what is the essence of this feature, and what are we deleting that isn't it?"

### Watch the whole movie and put every screen on one wall
Level: 2
- "When we did that, we realized there was this two-hour movie, and only 20 minutes were in the home." — [Masters of Scale](https://mastersofscale.com/brian-chesky/), 2017-05-03 — primary (transcript)
- "Then we asked the team, "Every single screen a user sees—put it on one wall." It turns out there are 150 screens." — [Figma Config 2023 conversation](https://www.figma.com/blog/config-brian-chesky-airbnb/), 2023-08-10 — primary (Figma's published transcript)
- "Designers should not be just focused on surfaces, they should be focused on user flows." — same conversation — primary (Figma's published transcript)

Ask it as: "Which screens exist today for parent, child and teacher, and where on that wall does this idea land? Which are new?" "What happens in the child's week outside our product, and does our flow ignore it?"

### Great tools for one side make the other side's experience
Level: 2
- "to create a great guest experience, you need great hosts, and to have great hosts, they need great tools." — [Lenny's Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4ef0juAMqoE), 2023-11-12 — primary (transcript)
- "It turns out that almost everything involves connecting the guest and host. When you have separate teams, they tend to have separate roadmaps that go in separate directions that become incompatible." — same episode — primary (transcript)

Ask it as: "What must a teacher have in hand for the parent to see the value? Which feature touches parent and teacher at once, and who owns that screen?"

### The peak: leave the comfort zone, come back changed
Level: 4
- "By day two or three, you need to have a challenge out of your comfort zone. If you do not leave your comfort zone, you do not remember the trip." — [Masters of Scale](https://mastersofscale.com/brian-chesky/), 2017-05-03 — primary (transcript)
- "If you can belong out of your comfort zone and something new happens to you, then there's going to be a moment of transformation where the person you were in a small way dies and a new, better version of yourself is reborn." — same episode — primary (transcript)

Ask it as: "What is the moment in a child's first month where they stretch past comfortable and come back changed? Would the parent see it? What is the last frame the family remembers at the end of term?"

### The failure end: make the principled decision
Level: 4
- "The first is I decided to not make business decisions, but to make a principled decision." — [Sequoia Crucible Moments](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/crucible-moments-airbnb), 2023-09-07 — primary (transcript)
- "How do I wanna be remembered, regardless of if I can predict the outcome?" — same episode — primary (transcript; the 2011 trashed-home crisis, answered with an open letter and a guarantee)

Ask it as: "A teacher no-shows, a child cries mid-lesson, a payment fails: write the failure frame for each. What would we be proud to be remembered for doing, before we know what it costs?"

### Earn permission to build new things
Level: cut
- "We can't do new things unless we have permission, and we don't have permission to work on new things until people love our core service." — [Figma Config 2023 conversation](https://www.figma.com/blog/config-brian-chesky-airbnb/), 2023-08-10 — primary (Figma's published transcript)
- "If they're complaining on social media, and they're calling customer service, they don't love our core service, so we have to get our house in order first." — same conversation — primary (Figma's published transcript)

Ask it as: "Do families love the core lesson yet? Name one real family and what they said. Is this new thing a way to avoid fixing that?"

### Hypothesis before test; ship only what you're proud of
Level: assumptions
- "I would try to make sure if you are going to do an A/B test or experiment, it should be hypothesis-driven. That if it works, you should be able to say why, not just what." — [Figma Config 2023 conversation](https://www.figma.com/blog/config-brian-chesky-airbnb/), 2023-08-10 — primary (Figma's published transcript)
- "You should only ship something that you're proud of. Don't test something until after you're happy." — same conversation — primary (Figma's published transcript)

Ask it as: "Write each belief as a claim that can fail: if it works, why? Which have we checked with a real family, and which are still guesses?"

### What we won't chase
Level: 1
- "I think that you should ask like if you never existed, what would be different about the world? Like what is your unique imprint to do?" — [Masters of Scale Summit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jo4Ze2c_hAY), 2025-10-16 — primary (video captions)
- "I say this because I think a lot of people like to chase trends. And by the way, by the time it's a trend sometimes it's late, right?" — same talk — primary (video captions)

Ask it as: "If this product never existed, what would be different for a K-8 child? Which trend are we tempted to chase that we will say no to out loud?" End the run: Keep / Cut / Defer.
