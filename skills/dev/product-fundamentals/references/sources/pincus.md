### Instinct vs idea
Level: 1
- "we have these instincts like in our gut, these human level instincts that are pretty much always right. And then we put these ideas on top of the instincts that are usually wrong." (LENNY [4:24], 2026-06-14, primary)
- "After decades of building products, I've learned that your instinct is probably right, but your idea is probably wrong." (Book passage quoted in GB, 2026-06-23, secondhand: I have not read the book itself.)
- "Travis Went and, did my idea. No, he did my instinct." (SUMMATION, 2025-01-28, primary. He had the taxi-by-phone instinct in 2003 and scrapped a small idea; Uber kept the instinct and changed the idea.)

Ask it as: "Set the idea aside. What do you believe about parents, children or teachers that you would still believe if this feature never shipped?" / "If this idea dies tomorrow, what instinct do we keep, and what is the next-closest idea that serves it?"

### Kill hope
Level: assumptions
- "Kill hope before hope kills you. It's there's a difference between belief and hope." (LENNY [33:37], 2026-06-14, primary. He says he can't claim to have coined it.)
- "Hope is confidence without basis." (LENNY [33:56], 2026-06-14, primary)
- "if you're just building this product and hoping they will come, hoping it'll spread virally or word of mouth, you know, that's hope strategy, you know, not a belief strategy." (LENNY [1:05:31], 2026-06-14, primary)

Ask it as: "For each line on the assumptions list, is there evidence from a real family, or are we hoping? Mark every hope unchecked." / "How will the first 50 families hear about this, and what has already proven that channel works for parents of K-8 kids?"

### B+ is the enemy of A
Level: cut
- "If you're asking whether or not your product is an A, it's not an A." (LENNY [58:10], 2026-06-14, primary)
- "The power is knowing it's a B+." (LENNY [59:23], 2026-06-14, primary. "B+ is the enemy of A" is my compression, not his phrase.)
- "It can deliver false signals in the form of B-plus products that never get to A-plus." (ENT, 2026-07-13, primary, about copying a proven product and getting positive signs from a weak new idea.)

Ask it as: "Do parents and teachers love this already, or are we asking whether it is good enough? If we are asking, it goes on the Cut or Defer list." / "What would an A look like for one family, and which family has shown signs of it?"

### Proven / Better / New: the sequence
Level: 2, 3
- "Start with enough "Proven" that your product doesn't fail for the wrong reasons. Find "Better" if you can, because it will increase your odds of success." (ENT, 2026-07-13, primary)
- "Proven is on this platform for this audience for this experience." (LENNY [14:06], 2026-06-14, primary. He warns founders cite any popular thing as "proven".)
- "we took the proven table layout and game mechanics from online poker, made it better by removing the download, and added a new feature: real people, often your friends, with their pictures at the table." (ENT, 2026-07-13, primary)

Ask it as: "Which existing product does a parent already use to find, book or pay for a music teacher, and which screens of it are we copying unchanged? Name the product and the screen." / "Which of our screens is proven on this platform for this audience, and which is new? Mark every new screen."

### Better: the 10-of-10 test
Level: 3
- ""Better" refers to something in the Proven product that you can do better in the eyes of the current audience, so that 10 out of 10 of those users will say, "Yeah, that's better!"" (ENT, 2026-07-13, primary)
- "10 out of 10 not you. What you think is better is called new, right?" (LENNY [8:41], 2026-06-14, primary)
- "I've always found you lose 80% to 90% of your audience when you require a download." (ENT, 2026-07-13, primary. His example of a Better: remove a step.)

Ask it as: "Which step in the flow would ten of ten parents we have watched call better? Who did we ask, or are we the one voting?" / "How many taps stand between a parent landing and a booked first lesson, and which one would we remove?"

### All new fails
Level: 4, assumptions
- "One of my maxims inside Zynga, repeated to this day, was "all New fails."" (ENT, 2026-07-13, primary)
- "You have to try many, many variants of each new idea and many new ideas and look in much smaller atomic units of innovation for new." (KP show-notes pull quote, 2026, primary)
- "The disciplined product maker knows to isolate the new zone to derisk their product." (ENT, 2026-07-13, primary)

Ask it as: "What is the single new thing in this idea? Strip it out: is what remains still something a parent would pay for?" / "If the new thing fails, name two other variants we would try next. If we cannot, we are attached to the idea."

### Cheapest test of desire, and the minimum idea state
Level: assumptions
- "We need a failure machine at the top of the funnel. We need to get to a minimum idea state that gets vibe coded." (KP pull quote, 2026, primary. This is the source of "minimum idea state"; it is not from the 2009 Stanford talk.)
- "And we just kept testing until we got a lot of clicks on something. And we didn't build anything until it got, you know, huge." (ALTUCHER [4:52], 2026-06-25, primary. He put text links in the poker game; the click led to a 404 page.)
- Stanford eCorner, 2009-10-28: Zynga tested "with a round of "ghetto testing" – five words that will be used to market the game, posted to the website live for five minutes." (Stanford's description, https://purl.stanford.edu/wn512qb0563, secondhand; I could not read the transcript.)

Ask it as: "What is the cheapest thing a parent could click, sign up for or pay for this week that would show they want this, before any code exists?" / "Which five consenting adults, parents or teachers we know, can we put the one-line promise in front of by Friday, and what would they have to do (not say) for us to call it desire?"

### Retention before reach: one repeat behaviour
Level: 4, assumptions
- "our core metric was retention not virality." (LENNY [43:58], 2026-06-14, primary)
- "if you don't think about why someone would use this in a year, if you don't have that mentality, it turns out when someone tries our product, they think that way too." (LENNY [44:39], 2026-06-14, primary)
- "It's about can we put in something that gets them to use us all the time every day." (SUMMATION, 2025-01-28, primary. On support.com: sales took off when the tool was applied to every ticket, not 10% of them.)

Ask it as: "What is the one thing a family does again and again (a lesson rebooked, a practice day logged) that tells us they will still be here in a year? Which of the three parties does it first?" / "Name the repeat act in the real world, not minutes in the app. What stops a family doing it after lesson three?"

### Bold Beats
Level: 4
- "every quarter you have to do a bold beat, which is a positive disruption in the consumer experience that, if it's successful, lights up Twitter or the blogosphere or TikTok—and moves some real metrics by 10% or more." (FORTUNE, 2026-08-20, primary)
- "in order to invent bold beats—innovations that really advance the social gaming experience for our players." (S-1, 2011-07-01, primary)
- "How do I add a whole new dimension to it?" (LENNY [42:37], 2026-06-14, primary. The question a bold beat answers when a proven product has gone boring.)

Ask it as: "What is the one moment in a child's first month that a parent would describe to another parent? Is it a new dimension on a proven lesson, or a feature bolted on?" / "What would the peak moment be, and what real number would move for our families if it worked?"

### Start embarrassingly small, judge by the user's eyes
Level: 1
- "by the time I got to Zingga I did something that was embarrassingly small." (LENNY [26:41], 2026-06-14, primary)
- "I tried to do everything with tribe. It was really ambitious and I didn't pick one use case" (LENNY [26:21], 2026-06-14, primary. Tribe's failure.)
- "if you define your ambition in the eyes of your consumer, not your peers, you're not trying to win awards and respect from your peers." (LENNY [16:17], 2026-06-14, primary)

Ask it as: "Who is the one parent whose delight we are chasing this quarter, and which use case are we refusing to chase for her?" / "What is the embarrassingly small version of this that still makes her glad she picked us?"

## Do not import

Zynga mechanics that would be manipulative toward children or parents. Pincus reports each as a success; do not read his results as a licence.
- Gifting-for-reciprocity loops: he built a metric on "You gifted them and they gifted you back" (LENNY [46:43]). A child or parent should never owe a gift or a turn to keep a streak.
- Energy bars and expiring timers: Zynga staples. I found no Pincus quote on them, so this is my flag, not his words. A countdown pressures a parent's wallet and a child's attention.
- Friend-obligation prompts: he concedes the games "took over the feeds" until "your friend who was playing it started to bug you" (LENNY [41:05]). Never message a classmate or a relative on a child's behalf.
- Time-in-app as a target: the S-1 boasts users "spend 2 billion minutes a day with our service." Our success is a lesson attended and a skill gained, not minutes. Use retention as a real-world act, never session time.
- Anything that makes a child recruit: "you and a friend" early-access keys sold $19M (LENNY [38:59]). A child is not a growth channel, and COPPA makes it worse.
- Scarcity and "heat" as monetisation: he turned marketing into "scarcity and, you know, heat driving feature" (LENNY [39:20]). Do not manufacture urgency for parents who pay.
- His own caution about his early methods: "I did every horrible thing in the book to, just to get revenues right away" (TechCrunch, 2009-11-06, https://www.techcrunch.com/2009/11/06/zynga-scamville-mark-pinkus-faceboo/, primary, from his own talk).

A/B tests are meaningless at about 27 paying families. His "once 22.8% click, 22.8% always click on it" (ALTUCHER [7:08]) holds only "with enough numbers." Here a cheap test means a few real, consenting adults, each shown the thing and watched. Zynga's 25 per cent click threshold is not our bar.
