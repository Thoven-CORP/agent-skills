# Approved Patterns — Reference Examples

**Reference material, not templates to clone.** These are opener and closer shapes that landed for specific prospects. Use them to see what kinds of shapes work — do NOT mechanically apply them to new prospects who don't fit.

The meta-rule (from SKILL.md): **the middle of the email is fixed; the opener and closer are where you write for THIS prospect, fresh, from their actual reality, not from a canonical menu.**

**How to use this file:**
- At Step 6.7, scan briefly. If a prior draft cleanly fits, surface it as reference.
- If no obvious fit exists, skip this file — write fresh and add the new pattern after the draft is sent.
- Do NOT force a fit. Most prospects will be new-pattern cases.

**Prospect type tags** (for organization, not for pattern-matching):

| Tag | Profile |
|-----|---------|
| `instructor-marketplace` | Built a platform where instructors/creators sell skills (Craftsy, Coursera, Maven) |

| `serial-founder-mentor` | Multiple exits but no specific domain overlap |

## Specific-Challenge Closer — Guidance, NOT a Lookup Table

When a real Thoven challenge maps to a prospect's actual experience, naming the challenge specifically can make the closer land. When it doesn't, the generic "learn from your journey" close is fine.

**Reason per prospect, fresh:**
- What is this prospect's *actual* relevant experience? (Not their type — their specific work.)
- What concrete Thoven problem would they have direct insight on?
- If the answer is "their experience is too distant to map cleanly" — use the generic close. Don't manufacture a challenge.

The specificity is what makes a challenge-reference work. "Cold-starting the teacher supply side before student volume was there" is real. "Challenges of scaling" is filler. If you can't generate something at the first level of specificity, default to the generic close.

**Keep it tight, but do NOT freeze it into one stem (updated 2026-05-20).** The "less rambling, more direct" lesson still holds — name the topic, don't spell out every mechanic. But Andres flagged that "One thing I'm working through is [topic], and..." had become a deterministic stem applied to every prospect, which is its own kind of robotic. The closer is intelligence-driven within an approved shape, not a fill-in-the-blank.

- **Too long (still rejected):** *"One thing I'm working through is getting teachers to actually use the live lesson layer instead of defaulting to Zoom, building real stickiness around the learning experience, not just the scheduling."*
- **Over-templated (also rejected now):** the same "One thing I'm working through is X, and I think you'd have a great read on it" stem on all ten drafts.
- **Right — pick an approved shape and vary it:** founder-journey + topic (*"I'm still early in my founder journey, and given your background I'd love the chance to connect. I think you'd have great insights on [topic]."*), or topic-value + connect (*"I think you'd have great insights on building the demand side, and I'd love to connect and get your perspective."*), or the collapsed generic.

See `feedback_closer_ask_collapse_pattern.md` for the full set of approved shapes and the non-determinism principle.

## `instructor-marketplace` Patterns

### instructor-marketplace — 2026-05-17
**Reference prospect:** John Levisay (Craftsy)
**Opener:**
> "I came across what you built at Craftsy and there's a ton of overlap with what I'm building. Wanted to reach out."

**Closer specific ask:**
> "As a founder early in my journey in music EdTech, I'd love to learn from your experience building Craftsy — especially how you approached the instructor side of that marketplace."

**When to use:** Prospect built a literal instructor marketplace (instructors sell skills, learners subscribe or pay per class) in a different creative domain. The product structure IS the same — only the domain differs. "There's a ton of overlap" is verified Andres language for this case. Do NOT use this opener for adjacent-shape prospects (see Steve Martocci — Splice is NOT instructor marketplace overlap).

**Key lesson:** "There's a ton of overlap" lands harder than "resonates" when the overlap is genuinely structural. The specificity of the closer (instructor side of the marketplace specifically) is where the personalization lives.

## `music-industry` Patterns

### music-industry — 2026-05-17
**Reference prospect:** Steve Martocci (Splice)
**Honest angle:** Splice serves music producers/beatmakers — NOT a teacher marketplace. No product overlap exists.
**Correct framing:** Investor perspective angle. Martocci invests in music + creator space; Codecademy investment signals EdTech learning thesis.
**Opener (approved):**
> "I came across what you built at Splice and your background in music, and wanted to reach out. As someone who's been building and now investing in the space, I think you'd have a lot of perspective on what I'm working on."

**When to use:** Prospect has music background BUT their product served producers/artists, not teachers or learners. Do NOT claim product overlap. Use the investor/perspective angle honestly. The hook is their dual role (builder + active investor), not Splice's features.

**Key lesson (critical):** Do NOT use "there's a ton of overlap" for music-industry prospects where the overlap is NOT real. The Splice case is the documented failure of this. Test: did their product serve music teachers, music students, or families? If no → music-industry honest angle only.

### music-industry:perspective-only — 2026-05-17
**Reference prospect:** Kevin Ohashi (Soundcloud monetization API, Spotify VP)
**Context:** Music background (recorded music distribution/monetization side — labels, rights holders, artists). NO product overlap with teacher marketplace. NO confirmed angel investment activity.

**Key gap:** The Steve Martocci pattern uses "building and now investing in the space" — this requires confirmed active angel history. Do NOT use that framing for corporate execs or VPs with no documented angel activity.

**Correct framing:** Pure perspective/mentor angle — lived experience inside the music platform economy gives them insight on the teacher-side of music, even if their product never served teachers directly.

**Opener shape (no investment angle):**
> "I came across your background in music and what you built at [company], and wanted to reach out. I think your experience building for the music industry could give you a lot of perspective on what we're working on at Thoven."

**When to use:** Prospect has music industry background (streaming, distribution, labels, artist tools) but:
- Their product did NOT serve music teachers, students, or families
- There is NO confirmed angel investment history

Use the "perspective" framing (lived experience inside music economy) rather than the "builder + investor" dual-role framing. No overlap claim. No investment angle. Just honest: their vantage point on the music industry is genuinely relevant to what Thoven is building.

**Also flag:** If batch-enrich shows "overlap: yes" for this prospect type, the flag is almost certainly wrong — verify it yourself before using it.

## Hook Structure — Locked 2026-05-18

**Rule:** The opener has three jobs only — WHY (the connection), WHAT (general overlap — NEVER describe Thoven), ASK setup. Middle paragraph owns the Thoven description.

**Locked pattern (Greg Gunn / Sidharth Kakkar / Pramod Sharma — all approved 2026-05-18):**

```
I came across [one specific anchor about prospect], and there's a lot of overlap with what I'm working on. [One-line rare-combo/perspective plant about THIS prospect specifically — what makes their perspective distinctive]. So I wanted to reach out.
```

**Anti-patterns to remove (every opener):**
1. Describing Thoven ("I'm building a marketplace for X" / "a digital-native music academy that does Y") — the middle paragraph already says this
2. Describing the prospect's company/background back to them
3. Citing specific research artifacts ("I found your 2019 podcast" / "specifically what you built with X")
4. Surgical parallels ("you built X for Y so I built X for Z")

**Real examples:**

- **Greg Gunn:** *"I came across what you built at Wireless Generation and what you do at Lingo, and there's a lot of overlap with what I'm working on. You have an interesting perspective — both building products for kids and scaling a K-12 EdTech company, and now being an investor. So I wanted to reach out."*

- **Sidharth Kakkar:** *"I came across what you built at Freckle and your investment in Tutored by Teachers, and there's a lot of overlap with what I'm working on. You're one of the few people who's both built a K-12 EdTech company at scale and has experience from an investor perspective. So I wanted to reach out."*

- **Pramod Sharma:** *"I came across what you built at Osmo, and there's a lot of overlap with what I'm working on. You're one of the few founders to build a K-8 learning product that families actually bought at scale, so I wanted to reach out."*

## Known-Company Opener Lead-Ins — Locked 2026-06-19

**Rule:** The opener verb has to match how recognizable the company is (see humanizer rule 31). For a **real / well-known company**, "I came across [Company]" reads as fake discovery (nobody "comes across" a household name), and "I've watched / I've seen what you've built" reads as if you studied them. Both are wrong. Use a lead-in that (a) respects the company's stature, (b) implies the person is *current* in the role, and (c) stays 2-3 words before the company name.

**Locked known-company lead-ins (Andres-approved 2026-06-19):**
- **"I saw what you built at [Company]…"** — simple past, implies they're still in it, no studied-them vibe. Default.
- **"I saw you're one of the people behind [Company]…"** — for co-founders / "one of the people," puts them in the role.
- **"I've followed [Company] for a while…"** — for a company Andres personally knows well (used for Maven).

Alternate the first two across a batch so multiple known-company openers don't read identically.

**After the lead-in, the locked hook body continues as normal:** `…, and there's a lot of overlap with what I'm working on. So I wanted to reach out.`

**Real examples (batch 2026-06-19):**
- *"I saw you're one of the people behind Skillshare, and there's a lot of overlap with what I'm working on. So I wanted to reach out."* (Malcolm Ong)
- *"I saw what you built at Thinkific, and there's a lot of overlap with what I'm working on. So I wanted to reach out."* (Miranda Lievers)
- *"I saw what you built at Shipt and Landing, and there's a lot of overlap with what I'm working on. So I wanted to reach out."* (Bill Smith)
- *"I've followed Maven for a while, and a lot of what you built there resonates with what I'm working on. I wanted to reach out."* (Shreyans Bhansali)

**Still use "I came across what you built at [Company]"** ONLY for genuinely lesser-known companies where real discovery is plausible (e.g., Class Companion). That's the Greg/Pramod/Osmo locked form below — it does NOT apply to household-name companies.

**Banned for known companies:** "I came across [famous company]", "I've watched what you've built", "I've seen what you've built" (all rejected 2026-06-19).

## Collapsed Closer + Ask Paragraph — Locked 2026-05-18

**Rule:** The closer and canonical ask line are collapsed into ONE paragraph instead of the verbatim two-paragraph template. **This intentionally deviates from the strict-template ask line.**

**Locked pattern (Greg / Sidharth / Pramod / Eli — all approved 2026-05-18):**

```
I think you'd have great insights into what I'm building and could provide a ton of value and advice. If you have time in the next couple of weeks, I'd love to connect, show you what we're building, and get your feedback.
```

**Critical rules:**
- **NO "Given your background" prefix.** Drop ALL "Given..." prefixes ("Given your experience," "Given what you've built," etc.). Start directly with substance. (Retro-fit to Greg + Sidharth + Pramod drafts 2026-05-18.)
- **NO "chat" — use "connect"** (existing rule, still applies).
- **The website line + sign-off stay verbatim AFTER this collapsed paragraph.**

**Per-prospect Notes stamp:** When using this collapsed pattern, note in Notion: `"Intentional deviation from verbatim template: ask line modified per locked pattern — 'connect, show you what we're building, and get your feedback' replaces canonical '20-30 minutes... connect'."`

Full rules: `memory/feedback_closer_ask_collapse_pattern.md`, `memory/feedback_drop_given_your_background.md`
