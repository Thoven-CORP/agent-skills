## 7. Erica Wenger — VC-Nature Prospect Pattern (Park Rodeo)

**Prospect:** GP at Park Rodeo (seed fund). Author of "Elephants Not Unicorns" thesis (long-duration, purpose-driven founders over rocket-ship unicorn chasing).
**Channel:** Email (sent 2026-04-17)
**Subject:** [TBD]

**Pattern demonstrated:** When the prospect is a **fund investor** (VC / GP) rather than a traditional operator-angel, run the VC-nature variant: lead with their published thesis or fund content, wrap product in thesis language, offer an advisor-AND-investor frame at the close. Skip the "learning from your journey" ask — they invest for a living; the ask is connection and pitch intro.

**Quality:** Strong

**Full draft (sent 2026-04-17):**
```
Hi Erica,

Your content has been all over my feed lately and I just finished reading your Elephants Not Unicorns post. What you describe really resonates with how I'm thinking about what I'm building.

My name is Andres Martinez, and a couple months ago I started building Thoven, a digital-native music academy with the mission of making learning music online more inspiring and accessible for families while making it easier for any musician and music teacher to teach and share their passion. We currently have 80 teachers onboarded and 50 MAU, with advisors from the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, Khan Academy, and Clever, and we're raising a $300k pre-seed with $50k already committed ($25k from family and friends, $25k from an angel investor).

I think we fit squarely into the elephant thesis, especially given what we're building toward. Our whole mission is transforming how music education is experienced and delivered, building something purpose-driven for families and the music community.

Would love to get the chance to introduce what I'm building and get your perspective as an investor and as a founder.

If you have time in the next couple weeks, would love to chat. In the meantime, you can check out our website at thoven.co. Thanks in advance!

Best,
Andres Martinez
```

**Key moves:**
- Opener cites recent thesis + current visibility: *"Your content has been all over my feed lately and I just finished reading your Elephants Not Unicorns post. What you describe really resonates with how I'm thinking about what I'm building."*
- Self-intro line *"My name is Andres Martinez, and..."* (VC cold intros tolerate a direct self-intro; operator-angel drafts often skip it since the context is founder-to-founder)
- Middle paragraph uses updated raise: *"we're raising a $300k pre-seed with $50k already committed ($25k from family and friends, $25k from an angel investor)."*
- Thesis-wrap paragraph between middle and closer: *"I think we fit squarely into the elephant thesis, especially given what we're building toward. Our whole mission is transforming how music education is experienced and delivered, building something purpose-driven for families and the music community."*
- Closer reframes ask for VC: *"Would love to get the chance to introduce what I'm building and get your perspective as an investor and as a founder."* (not the operator-angel "learn from your journey" close)
- Sends the website at the end: *"In the meantime, you can check out our website at thoven.co."*

**Lesson — VC-nature prospect rules:**
- Lead hook = their **thesis or fund content or historical investments**, not career history
- Add a thesis-wrap paragraph that positions Thoven inside the fund's stated worldview (between middle paragraph and closer)
- Closer = "introduce what I'm building + perspective as an investor and founder" (NOT "learn from your journey")
- Drop the operator-angel advice framing — they read pitch decks for a living
- **Raise size intentionally larger for VC drafts.** Operator-angel default: $150k / $25k committed. VC default: $300k / $50k committed ($25k F&F + $25k angel). Rationale: VCs can write bigger checks by default, so the raise scales to match their minimum check expectation. Do NOT normalize the VC raise back down to the operator-angel number.
- Same sign-off: *"If you have time in the next couple weeks, would love to chat. Thanks in advance! Best, Andres Martinez"*
- Check Intro Path first — if a fund prospect has obvious portfolio competitors (e.g., Bubba Page with Flashlight Learning and Gabb Wireless), **deprioritize rather than pitch**

## 8. Shadiah Sigala — "Story Resonated" Opener (Story-Shape Pattern)

**Prospect:** Co-founder Kinside (childcare marketplace, acquired by UrbanSitter May 2025) + co-founder HoneyBook (unicorn, $2.4B). Active angel investor. Builds for families from lived experience.
**Channel:** Email: shadiah@kinside.com (candidate — may forward post-acquisition)
**Subject:** Early stage EdTech founder, would love your advice

**Pattern demonstrated:** When a prospect's founding story parallels yours (lived the problem, built for families), reference the *feeling* of what they built — not the specifics. Don't describe their company back at them. Let the parallel emerge naturally. Stop generating options and ask the user to write the opener if multiple AI-generated versions keep getting rejected — their words will be better.

**Quality:** Strong

**Key moves:**
- Names the company once (Kinside) but never describes what it does
- Does NOT quote the prospect directly
- Does NOT signal research depth ("I found your podcast")
- Does NOT list the parallel explicitly ("you built X, I build Y")
- Closer is specific: *"get your perspective on building for families"*

**Full opener (as sent):**
> I came across your background and a lot of your story resonated. Kinside has some overlap with what I'm building — the focus on families and how you got into it feel really close to our mission and how we started Thoven. I wanted to reach out to learn more.

**Lesson — "story shape" rule:**
The prospect knows what their story is — you don't need to explain it back to them. Just say the story felt close and name the shape (focus on families, how you got into it). Trust the reader to make the connection.

**Anti-patterns fixed during drafting:**
- "I found a Pomona podcast where you talked about building a company while becoming a parent..." → performative research signal
- "You built HoneyBook so creative small businesses could focus on their craft, and then a marketplace for parents." → describing her company back at her
- Multiple AI-generated opener options rejected before user wrote their own phrasing → correct response was to stop generating and let the user lead

## 9. John Levisay — Direct-Analog Opener ("There's a ton of overlap")

**Prospect:** Co-founder & CEO Craftsy (instructor-led creative arts education marketplace, $112M raised, sold to Comcast/NBCUniversal 2017). Active angel investor.
**Channel:** LinkedIn DM (linkedin.com/in/john-levisay-0056ab2/ — no email found)
**Subject:** Early stage EdTech founder, would love your advice

**Pattern demonstrated:** When a prospect built something that IS the closest historical model to Thoven (not adjacent, not parallel — literally same structure in a different creative domain), use "there's a ton of overlap" directly. This is verified Andres language (from Care.com/Sheila). Don't reach for more elaborate phrasing — the simple direct statement lands harder when the overlap is real.

**Opener anti-pattern check:** Two AI-generated openers were rejected before landing on Andres's actual phrasing:
- "it resonates a lot with what I'm building" → rejected as recycled phrase
- "to be honest, it's the closest thing I've seen" → rejected as not Andres's language

**Approved opener (Andres-confirmed 2026-05-17):**
> I came across what you built at Craftsy and there's a ton of overlap with what I'm building. Wanted to reach out.

**Key moves:**
- Names Craftsy once, no description of what it does
- "There's a ton of overlap" — direct, honest, verified Andres language
- "Wanted to reach out" — clean natural close, no filler
- Two sentences only — short, specific, not padded

**Closer:** Mentor-framed on instructor marketplace experience specifically:
> "As a founder early in my journey in music EdTech, I'd love to learn from your experience building Craftsy — especially how you approached the instructor side of that marketplace."

**Lesson:** For the CLOSEST analog prospect (same business model, different domain), don't soften or elaborate the opener. "There's a ton of overlap" is stronger than "resonates" or "is similar to" when it's genuinely true. Don't reach for uniqueness at the cost of natural language — the specificity of the closer is where the personalization lands.

**When to use:** Only when the prospect built literally the same business model. Do not apply "there's a ton of overlap" to adjacent-shape or music-adjacent prospects where overlap is not real (see Steve Martocci case).
